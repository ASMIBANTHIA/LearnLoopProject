
# LearnLoop: Helping Online Learners Decide What to Study Next

**Project type:** Product Discovery & Strategy  
**Role:** Product Manager (Self-directed case study)  
**Research:** Secondary research, competitor analysis and OULAD dataset analysis

## 1. The problem

Online learners may struggle to organise multiple learning tasks, manage deadlines and maintain consistent study habits.

LearnLoop explores how a personalised study-planning companion could help learners decide what to work on next.

## 2. Research approach

- Reviewed academic research on self-regulated learning and online education.
- Compared Google Classroom, Moodle, Coursera and Notion.
- Analysed the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset (OULAD).
- Examined engagement, assessment behaviour and student outcomes.

## 3. Key data insights

- 31.16% of student-course records ended in withdrawal.
- 37.02% of students who eventually withdrew had zero recorded VLE clicks in their first 28 days.
- The withdrawal rate was 79.88% among students with zero early activity, compared with 22.94% among students with some activity.
- This relationship appeared across all seven course modules, although its strength varied.

These are retrospective associations, not evidence that inactivity causes withdrawal.

## 4. Product opportunity

Help learners move from intending to study to actually completing manageable learning tasks.

**Target experience:** A learner opens LearnLoop and immediately understands what to study next and why.

## 5. Proposed solution

LearnLoop recommends the next learning task using:

- Deadline urgency
- Estimated effort
- Course priority
- Available study time
- Learning progress

The recommendation is explainable and can be overridden by the learner.

## 6. MVP

- Course and task management
- Study availability settings
- Personalised next-task recommendations
- Progress tracking

## 7. Success measurement

**North Star Metric:** Weekly Active Learning Days.

Supporting metrics:
- Task completion rate
- Recommendation acceptance rate
- Onboarding activation rate
- Overdue task rate
- User-reported workload stress

## 8. Experiment design

Proposed A/B test:

- Control: Standard deadline-sorted task list.
- Treatment: Personalised next-task recommendations.

Primary metric: Task completion rate.

Proposed duration: Four weeks, subject to sample-size and statistical-power calculations.

## 9. Limitations and next steps

- OULAD is historical data from one online university, not a dataset of learners using multiple platforms.
- The data does not contain learners' available study hours.
- The analysis identifies associations, not causal effects.
- The multi-course workload problem and proposed solution still require direct user interviews and usability testing.

The next phase would validate the problem with learners, prototype the recommendation experience and test it with real users.
