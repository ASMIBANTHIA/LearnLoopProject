# LearnLoop — Secondary Research

**Research Type:** Secondary Research  
**Project:** LearnLoop  
**Status:** Initial research  
**Purpose:** Understand challenges faced by students in online learning environments.

---

## 1. Research Objective

The objective of this research is to understand whether students managing online courses face challenges related to:

- Time management and study planning.
- Maintaining consistent learning engagement.
- Managing motivation and procrastination.
- Recovering after falling behind.

This research will help identify evidence-backed product opportunities for LearnLoop.

## 2. Research Findings

### Study 1: Self-Regulated Learning and Academic Achievement

**Authors:** Broadbent & Poon (2015)  
**Research:** A systematic review of 12 studies on online higher education.

**Key findings:**
- Time management was positively associated with academic achievement.
- Metacognition and effort regulation were also positively associated with academic outcomes.
- Critical thinking showed a positive association with achievement.
- The authors noted that these relationships were generally weaker than those observed in traditional classroom settings.

**LearnLoop implication:**

A study-planning feature could help students organise their learning activities and monitor their progress.

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.iheduc.2015.04.007

---

### Study 2: Self-Regulated Learning in MOOCs

**Authors:** Lee, Watson & Watson (2019)  
**Research:** Systematic literature review of self-regulated learning in Massive Open Online Courses.

**Key findings:**
- Self-regulated learning was identified as a positive factor in MOOC learning.
- Strategies included goal setting, time management and effort regulation.
- Motivation and help-seeking were also important areas.

**LearnLoop implication:**

The product should not only display course progress. It should also help students set manageable goals and organise their next learning activities.

**Source:** https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1211597

---

### Study 3: Why College Students Procrastinate in Online Courses

**Research:** Why college students procrastinate in online courses: A self-regulated learning perspective (2021).

**Sample:** 207 college students.

**Key findings:**
- Perceived relevance of course content and technology usability were indirectly related to procrastination.
- Task value and emotional cost played roles in these relationships.
- The research highlights that motivation and the perceived effort of a task matter.

**LearnLoop implication:**

A long list of incomplete tasks may not be enough.

Recommendations should make the next activity manageable and explain why it matters.

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.iheduc.2021.100807

---

### Study 4: Does Scheduling Alone Improve Online Learning?

**Authors:** Baker, Evans & Dee (2016)  
**Research:** Randomised experiment testing a scheduling nudge in a Massive Open Online Course.

**Sample:** 18,043 learners.

**Key findings:**
- Students were given an opportunity to schedule a time to watch lecture videos.
- The intervention did not improve near-term engagement.
- It had weakly significant negative effects on some longer-term engagement, persistence and performance outcomes.

**LearnLoop implication:**

This is an important caution.

Simply adding reminders or asking students to schedule study sessions may not be sufficient.

LearnLoop should investigate whether more useful support comes from:
- Helping students prioritise tasks.
- Considering available study time.
- Providing realistic catch-up plans.
- Making recommendations relevant to current academic commitments.

These are product hypotheses, not proven effects.

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858416674007

---

### Study 5: Supporting Self-Regulated Learning in Distance Education

**Research:** Supporting Self-Regulated Learning in Distance Learning Contexts at Higher Education Level (2022).

**Method:** Systematic literature review of 38 studies.

**Key findings:**
- Students benefit from support across planning, learning activities and reflection.
- Motivation, metacognition and emotional regulation are relevant to learning.
- Different interventions can support different stages of self-regulated learning.

**LearnLoop implication:**

The product should consider the complete learning cycle:

1. Planning what to study.
2. Completing learning activities.
3. Reviewing progress and adjusting the plan.

**Source:** https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.792422

## 3. Consolidated Research Insights

| Research theme | Evidence observed | Potential product opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| Time management | Associated with online academic achievement | Study planner |
| Goal setting | Identified as an important learning strategy | Weekly learning goals |
| Motivation | Related to procrastination and self-regulation | Manageable next-step recommendations |
| Engagement | Relevant to persistence and learning outcomes | Progress monitoring |
| Scheduling | Scheduling nudges alone may not improve outcomes | More contextual planning support |
| Reflection | Important part of self-regulated learning | Progress review and catch-up mode |

## 4. Initial Product Opportunities

Based on the research, we identify four opportunities to investigate:

**Opportunity 1: Unified academic workload**

Help students see activities and deadlines across their courses in one place.

**Opportunity 2: Prioritised learning activities**

Help students understand which task deserves attention based on urgency, effort and academic importance.

**Opportunity 3: Manageable study goals**

Help students break their workload into realistic activities.

**Opportunity 4: Learning recovery**

Help students revise their plans after periods of inactivity or missed study sessions.

## 5. What We Know vs What We Assume

### Supported by existing research
- Time management is associated with online learning outcomes.
- Self-regulated learning strategies are relevant to online learning.
- Motivation and task value are relevant to procrastination.
- Scheduling interventions do not automatically improve engagement.

### Still unvalidated hypotheses
- Students specifically want one dashboard for multiple online platforms.
- Students struggle to prioritise tasks across different courses.
- A catch-up feature would be preferred over ordinary reminders.
- Students would consistently use personalised next-task recommendations.

These assumptions require further investigation.

## 6. Research Limitations

- The studies examine different populations and learning environments.
- Some research concerns MOOCs rather than university students enrolled in multiple independent courses.
- Associations between learning strategies and academic performance do not establish causation.
- Published research cannot replace direct feedback from our target users.

## 7. Conclusion

Existing research provides a reasonable foundation for investigating time management, self-regulated learning, motivation and engagement as product problem areas.

LearnLoop will explore whether combining academic workload visibility, task prioritisation and learning recovery can address these challenges.

The next stage will use competitor analysis and the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset (OULAD) to investigate student behaviour and refine our product hypotheses.
