
# LearnLoop — Public User Evidence

## Objective
Understand common problems experienced by online learners using publicly available user feedback.

## Research sources
- App Store and Google Play reviews of online learning apps.
- Reddit discussions about online learning, procrastination and study planning.
- Public reviews of productivity and task-management tools.
- Existing academic research on online learning behaviour.

## Evidence to collect

For each relevant observation, record:

| Source | User problem | Existing workaround | Unmet need |
|---|---|---|---|
| Link to source | What difficulty does the user describe? | What do they currently do? | What remains unresolved? |

## Research questions
1. Do learners struggle to organise multiple courses?
2. Do learners have difficulty deciding what to study next?
3. What causes learners to fall behind?
4. What tools do learners currently use?
5. What frustrations remain unresolved?

## Limitations
Public reviews and online discussions are self-selected feedback. They do not represent all learners and cannot establish how common a problem is.



## Findings from public evidence

### 1. Managing multiple online courses can feel overwhelming

A learner balancing full-time work with two online courses described struggling with assignments, discussion boards, quizzes and competing deadlines. Existing planners and reminders were not enough to make the workload manageable.

Source: [Reddit — Struggling with multiple online classes](https://www.reddit.com/r/OnlineClassHelp_/comments/1np6k2s)

**Product implication:** A consolidated view of learning tasks and deadlines may help learners understand their overall workload.

### 2. Learners struggle with inconsistent study schedules

A qualitative study of online university students identified different approaches to time management. Some learners maintained regular study schedules, while others struggled with unpredictable availability, work and family responsibilities.

Source: [Persistence and time challenges in an open online university](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41239-022-00338-6)

**Product implication:** Study plans should accommodate changing availability rather than assume learners have fixed study hours.

### 3. Falling behind makes it harder to manage coursework

A learner studying online described falling behind on lectures, readings and assignments after illness, with multiple weeks of unfinished work accumulating.

Source: [Reddit — Falling behind in online studies](https://www.reddit.com/r/GetStudying/comments/1rx3f3n/im_behind_by_2_3_weeks_how_do_i_catch_up_on/)

**Product implication:** A catch-up experience could help learners identify what needs attention first instead of facing an overwhelming list of unfinished work.

### Research limitations

- Reddit posts are individual experiences, not representative surveys.
- The academic study provides qualitative evidence, not population-wide prevalence estimates.
- These sources support the existence of planning and consistency challenges, but do not prove demand for LearnLoop specifically.
- The proposed solution still requires usability testing.



## 5. Competitive positioning

### The gap LearnLoop aims to address

Existing products help learners organise courses, manage tasks, track deadlines and monitor progress.

LearnLoop focuses on a different question:

**"Given my available study time, deadlines and unfinished coursework, what should I work on next?"**

### Proposed differentiation

1. **Workload-aware recommendations:** Recommend tasks based on available study time and estimated effort.
2. **Cross-course prioritisation:** Help learners compare competing tasks across their manually added courses.
3. **Explainable decisions:** Explain why a task is recommended instead of presenting an unexplained priority score.
4. **Flexible planning:** Adjust recommendations when availability or deadlines change.

### Positioning statement

For online learners managing multiple courses, LearnLoop is a study-planning companion that turns competing deadlines and unfinished coursework into a realistic next action.

Unlike general-purpose task managers, LearnLoop is designed around learning priorities and study availability.

### Competitive limitation

These capabilities are a proposed differentiation, not a proven competitive advantage. Existing tools may support similar workflows through customisation. LearnLoop must demonstrate that its learning-specific recommendation experience is easier and more useful.
